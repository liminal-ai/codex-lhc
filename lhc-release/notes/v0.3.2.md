Source: `0b379e0de761a5e6252088a6e4ecffd35c7e56e7` (plus the v0.3.2 version-bump commit at promotion)
LHC SDK pin: `f4de85c298bb76cbe660a2d717c9aa77b5599b8e` (on the shared LHC main line, merge `e0077f3`)

Supersedes: v0.3.1 (`6f849b7b3e`)

## Summary

v0.3.2 makes long-running sessions durable through compaction as a matter of design, not luck. The compact-gating campaign removed every internal refusal that could strand a session at its most fragile moment, the upstream base moved to alpha.20, and three defects found across two rounds of real cold-thread burn-in — invalid paginated rollout rewrites, a history projection that could not survive a rollout replacement, and nonsensical continuation timestamps — are fixed and proven.

## Compaction never strands a session

The governing principle, applied across six reviewed stories (LIM-101 through LIM-106): nothing stops compact. Conditions that previously refused, vetoed, or rolled back a compaction now detect, warn, and recover forward.

- **Mid-turn compaction proceeds** through input arrival, degraded capture, stale history, transient startup state, hook vetoes, and bounded worker timeouts (LIM-101).
- **Installs and rollout rewrites are forward-only.** Once compact work completes, recovery bookkeeping, stale claims, cancellation races, or a failed recorder reopen can no longer suppress or undo an installed compact. The durable compact record is the rollout itself; the archive marker is write-behind duplication with a bounded commit budget (LIM-102).
- **Compact body assembly degrades instead of stranding.** Missing or failed derivations fall back through stored material; the session continues with a truthful warning (LIM-103).
- **Startup reconciliation warns through ambiguity.** A stale or damaged rollout is regenerated from the thread's installed view; an exact active tool call/result suffix survives byte-stably; ambiguous alignment preserves prior bytes and converges instead of failing (LIM-104, including occurrence-aware ordered-prefix replay).
- **The SDK no longer refuses recoverable compacts.** Escalated-success outcomes finalize as complete, and retry-budget exhaustion terminalizes cleanly instead of retrying without bound (LIM-105).
- **Certification canaries** exercise the full compact arm end to end, including an ACK-write-failure fault seam, and host-validation reload vetoes are demoted to warnings (LIM-106).

## Burn-in defect fixes

Two rounds of real cold-thread burn-in (correlated tool calls, production compact bands, restart and resume) surfaced three defects, all fixed here:

- **Paginated rollout rewrites now emit valid top-level ordinals.** During pre-release burn-in, every Smart Compact rewrite of a paginated rollout in the alpha.20 candidate omitted per-record ordinals, so the recorder rejected the rewritten file on reopen and entered recovery on every compact. This defect never shipped in a public release. Ordinal assignment is now a single canonical rule shared between the recorder and the rewrite path, honoring history-base and subagent boundaries; legacy rollouts remain ordinal-free. Proven by rewrite/reopen/next-append tests and four mutation proofs.
- **Rewritten rollouts carry an explicit generation identity, and history projection heals itself.** A second burn-in round found that after Smart Compact atomically replaced a paginated rollout, the separate thread-history projection kept the dead generation's frontier and failed every subsequent projection (including `thread/read` and shutdown) with "durable rollout shrank before projection." The projector now detects a generation swap by exact identity, atomically resets the projected turns, items, and frontier as one unit, and rebuilds from the new generation — including equal-size replacements and rewrites of subagent threads with lifted start ordinals. Each rewritten rollout's session-metadata record now carries a `rollout_generation_id` (a UUID minted per rewrite); ordinary appends never change it, and rollouts without one (all existing files) take a one-time safe rebuild path. Older Codex-LHC binaries ignore the new field: a v0.3.1-line runtime can read, resume, and append to a nonce-bearing rollout unchanged (proven directly against the preserved binary).
- **Compact-continuation receipts carry real timestamps.** The Rust SDK's continuation persistence fed epoch seconds into date math that expected epoch days, producing years near 4,895,372. One canonical UTC formatter (the already-correct implementation, now shared) replaces the duplicate. Ordinary event timestamps were never affected.

## Platform and infrastructure

- **Upstream advanced to `rust-v0.148.0-alpha.20`**, including the code-mode gRPC health endpoint, executor policy audit telemetry, and `codex doctor` storage diagnostics.
- **LHC SDK advanced** to include bounded large-message reads (mature threads past the SQLite bound-parameter ceiling no longer fail generic large reads) and the compact-continuation semantics above.

## Compatibility

- One internal thread-store migration: `thread_history_projection_state` gains a nullable `rollout_generation_id` column (migration 0005). Existing rows migrate as NULL and rebuild once, safely. v0.3.1 threads open unchanged under v0.3.2. (The upstream alpha.20 merge does include app-server/protocol API changes; see upstream release notes.)
- Rewritten rollout files add a top-level `rollout_generation_id` field to the session-metadata record only. Older binaries ignore it; rollback to v0.3.1 after a v0.3.2 compact remains safe (verified against the preserved binary).
- Existing legacy (non-paginated) rollout files remain readable unchanged. Rewritten legacy files remain ordinal-free, and like all rewrites they may carry the forward-compatible `rollout_generation_id` field on the session-metadata record, which older binaries ignore.
- Rollback boundary: rolling back to v0.3.1 after a v0.3.2 paginated compact leaves a rollout the older binary can still read; continuation receipts written by v0.3.2 keep normal timestamps under either binary.
